# ClaimLab dataset card

## Composition

Fifteen authored synthetic insurance scenarios: nine with generated images (five auto, four property) and six evaluated through policy-document retrieval. The expected distribution is five `covered`, four `excluded` and six `needs_review`. `excluded` includes losses outside the fictional purchased coverage.

There are no real claimant records, uploaded claimant photographs, real customer identities or recorded human calls. Images are generated by `gpt-image-2`; voices are AI-generated. Policy declarations, endorsement applicability and incident details are fictional assumptions. The dataset is for demonstration and workflow evaluation, not model training or binding insurance adjudication.

Source policy wording remains attributable to its publishers. Public availability does not establish an open-data license. This repository does not grant rights to third-party policy text. Full PDFs and their extracted corpus are not bundled; selected paraphrases and retrieved excerpts are retained to make the recorded judgments inspectable. Check publisher terms before redistributing or using source material beyond this demo.

## Files and fields

`scenarios.json` holds the full active fixture set. `examples/cases.json` contains three unchanged examples from it; these are a convenience sample, not an additional test split.

- `id`, `title`, `category`: stable identity and display labels.
- `claimant_facts`: private scenario facts supplied only to the claimant agent.
- `policy`, `policy_source`: selected terms, fictional declarations and source attribution.
- `expected`: frozen authored verdict and relevant clause IDs; not sent to the evaluated model.
- `has_image`, `image_prompt`: synthetic image-generation setup.
- `challenge`: descriptive metadata, not model evidence.

`public/data/results.json` preserves the actual interview, Astra assessment, generated-media references, visual observations, retrieved passages and document reviews. `public/data/decisions.json` contains the Decisions API predictions and the source evidence used for each request. `public/data/harbor-summary.json` contains the replay rewards.

## Data flow and leakage boundaries

The claimant uses private facts to answer naturally. Astra sees the actual conversation and relevant evidence, asks up to two follow-ups, and may leave facts unelicited. Decisions receives this collected evidence; it does not receive the expected label, private fact sheet or Astra's final coverage verdict. For document cases, retrieval uses declarations and actual interview text, not expected labels. Saved JSON contains both inputs and labels for inspection; consumers must preserve these boundaries when creating another evaluator.

## Limitations

The expected labels have not been reviewed by insurance experts. There is no train/test split, localization ground truth, calibrated confidence, repeated-rollout analysis or representative sampling of real claims. Retrieval can miss controlling terms. Policy sample inclusion does not establish endorsement applicability. An image cannot establish intent, dates, actor identity or whether a police report was made. Label agreement alone does not validate an explanation. The recorded voices preserve the saved dialogue and do not add coverage evidence.
